The app has no internet access

This is not a promise about intent, it is a property of the app you can check yourself. Android requires an app to declare every capability it wants in its manifest, and the operating system enforces that list. EasyLoop declares these and nothing else:

  • RECORD_AUDIO — the microphone
  • POST_NOTIFICATIONS — the notification shown while a session is running
  • FOREGROUND_SERVICE, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MEDIA_PLAYBACK, FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE — so audio keeps playing and recording when the screen is off

There is no INTERNET permission. Without it, Android blocks every network connection the app could attempt, so no recording, setting or identifier can be transmitted anywhere even by mistake. You can verify this on the app's Google Play listing under App permissions → See more, or in any APK inspector.

What EasyLoop stores, and where

Everything stays in the app's private storage on your device, which no other app can read:

  • Your recordings. The original audio captured from the microphone, kept untouched so any edit can be undone.
  • Your projects. Tempo, loop lengths, effects, volumes, positions, colours — the arrangement, stored as parameters next to the audio.
  • Your sound library. Individual sounds and whole circles you chose to keep.
  • Your settings. Theme, timing preferences, metronome, and the audio latency measured for your particular phone.

Files you export (WAV, MP3 or M4A) are written to the app's own folder under your device's shared storage, so that you can pass them to other apps. Once you share an export, whatever you shared it with handles it under its own rules, not ours.

Uninstalling EasyLoop deletes all of it. There is no copy anywhere else, because there is nowhere else.

The microphone

The microphone is opened only while a recording is running, and captures only the take you asked for. It is not used in the background, it is not used to listen while you are not recording, and nothing captured is analysed, uploaded or shared.

While a session is active the app runs a foreground service — the notification you can see in the status bar. That is what keeps playback and recording alive when you leave the app; it does not extend what the microphone is used for. Swiping EasyLoop out of the recents list closes the app and releases the microphone immediately.

What EasyLoop does not do

  • No account, no sign-in, no e-mail address required.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifier, no ad SDK.
  • No analytics, no usage statistics, no crash reporting service.
  • No contacts, no location, no photos, no calendar, no phone state.
  • No cookies or web tracking — the app has no web content.
  • No profile is built about you anywhere, by us or by anyone else.

Children

EasyLoop is a general-audience music app and is not directed at children under 13. Because it collects no data whatsoever, it also collects no data from children. If you are a parent and have a question about it, write to the address above.

Your rights

Data-protection law (including the GDPR and the UK GDPR) gives you rights of access, correction, erasure, portability and objection over personal data a company holds about you. In EasyLoop's case there is nothing to exercise them against: no personal data ever reaches us, so there is no record of you to hand over, correct or delete.

What you do have is direct control of everything the app stores, because it is all on your device:

  • Delete an individual recording, sound or project inside the app.
  • Settings → Restore default settings returns preferences to how the app ships.
  • Android's Settings → Apps → EasyLoop → Storage → Clear storage erases everything at once.
  • Uninstalling removes all of it permanently.

No legal basis for processing is listed here because no processing of personal data takes place. If that ever changes, this page changes with it — see below.

Data breaches

A breach requires data to be held somewhere it can be taken from. EasyLoop holds none: there is no server, no database and no account system associated with this app. The security of the files on your phone is handled by Android's app sandbox and by your device's own screen lock and encryption.

Planned features, and what would change

Two features on the roadmap would change what this page has to say. Neither is present in any released version, and this policy will be updated, and the change published here, before either of them ships:

  • Online collaboration. Playing together would require the sound files in a shared project to be uploaded so the other participants' devices can play them. That would need internet access, and this page would then describe what is uploaded, where it is stored and how to have it deleted.
  • A free version supported by ads. An ad network would collect device identifiers under its own privacy policy. The paid version would remain free of ads and of any ad SDK.

Until then, the answers in the table at the top of this page are the whole picture.
